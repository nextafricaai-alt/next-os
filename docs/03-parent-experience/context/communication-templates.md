# Communication Templates

> All parent-facing messages come from these. Adapt names and facts; keep structure and tone.

## 1. Incident notice (sent by MANAGER, same day, before/at pickup)
**Subject: [Child] — incident today, [he/she] is okay**
Dear [Parent], I want to tell you directly about something that happened today.
- What happened: [facts, time, where]
- How [Child] is now: [condition]
- What we did immediately: [first aid / comfort / observation]
- What we're changing so it doesn't repeat: [action]
I'm available tonight on [phone] if you'd like to talk. — [Manager]

## 2. Illness notice (child sent home / exclusion)
Dear [Parent], [Child] developed [symptom] at [time]. Per our health policy [he/she] needs to be picked up / rest at home. Return when: [criteria]. What we observed: [notes]. — [Manager]

## 3. Illness alert (class exposure, no names)
Dear parents, a child in [class] has been diagnosed with [illness]. Watch for: [symptoms]. Our steps: [cleaning/measures]. Your child can attend normally unless symptoms appear.

## 4. Welcome message (enrollment confirmed)
Dear [Family], welcome to [Center]! [Child]'s first day is [date]. What to bring: [...]. Your teacher is [name]. Our promise: you will hear from us every single day [Child] is with us. We're honored by your trust — and we'll care for [Child]'s heart as much as [his/her] growth.

## 5. Fee reminder (gentle → firm ladder)
- Day 3 overdue: friendly reminder with amount and how to pay.
- Day 10: direct reminder, offer a conversation if circumstances are hard.
- Day 21: manager call, payment plan template.

## 6. Event invite / term calendar
[Structure: what, when, why it matters for the children, RSVP]

## 7. Difficult conversation opener (developmental concern)
"We've been observing [Child] closely and love [specific strength]. We've also noticed [observation, not diagnosis] and wanted to think together with you..." — always in person or by call, never text-only.
